Basics

Money

The app supports USD, AED and IQD. USD is the base currency: every total you compare or report on is a US dollar figure.

A foreign-currency record always stores the rate that was used at that moment. Changing a rate in Settings never rewrites old records — that rate is only the value offered when you create something new.

Rates are written as units per one dollar. So 3.6725 means 3.6725 AED buys one dollar.

Draft and confirmed

Many documents start as a draft. A draft is visible but does not affect any balance, total or report. It only counts once you confirm it. Payments, transfers and revaluations all work this way.

Deactivate, not delete

Master records — persons, goods, categories, accounts — are never deleted. You deactivate them. They stop appearing in new documents, and every old document that used them stays exactly as it was.

Starting empty

A fresh install has no data at all. To explore with realistic figures, use Settings → Danger zone → Load sample data. Reset clears everything again.

Dashboard

The opening screen for Manager and Staff. It shows total outstanding, overdue, collected and invoiced, plus charts for money in against money out, product mix, contract status and how old the unpaid amounts are.

Every figure counts confirmed money only. A draft payment does not appear here.

CEO users see a separate Executive screen instead, with the same story at a higher level.

Persons

Everyone you deal with: buyers, suppliers, employees and others. This list used to be called Customers; the name was widened because it now holds more than customers.

Types

Employees and Others do not trade, so the form hides currency, payment terms, credit limit and portal access, and explains why. They never appear when you choose a party for a contract.

The person page

Four tabs: Contracts, Invoices, Claims and Payments. Invoices shows buying and selling together, because the same person can be both. Claims shows what was raised against them; it is tied to the invoice, so a claim can never appear under the wrong person.

Portal access

One person at a time can be linked to the customer portal. Turning it on for someone automatically turns it off for anyone else. Deactivating a person also removes their portal link.

Contracts

A contract is an agreement with one person to buy or sell goods. It holds one or more goods lines.

Pricing

Each goods line is priced from the metal exchange:

unit price = LME price × (LME percent ÷ 100) + premium
line value = unit price × quantity in tonnes

The LME percent reflects how much metal the material actually contains. The premium is what you add on top.

Prices belong to the deal, not the product

LME percent, premium and incoterm sit on the contract line, never on the goods record. The same copper sold to two buyers carries two different premiums.

Quantity limit

You cannot invoice more than the contract agreed. If you try, the message shows the contract quantity, what is already invoiced, what is left and what you asked for. Provisional invoices count towards the limit.

Containers

A container is a shipment record and nothing more. It holds a reference, load date, gross and net weight, and the goods inside.

It carries no money, no status and no contract link. How much has shipped is worked out from the invoices instead, so there is only one place that decides it.

Warehouse

Goods in and goods out. Each document is linked to a confirmed, priced invoice and moves stock into or out of one warehouse.

Stock per warehouse and per product is calculated from these documents. A document that would push stock below zero is refused.

Warehouses themselves are master records you can deactivate.

Purchase and Sale

Six document types, three on each side:

StagePurchaseSaleHas prices?
OrderPurchase orderSale orderNo
ProvisionalPurchase provisionalSale provisionalYes
FinalPurchase invoiceSale invoiceYes

The chain

An order becomes a provisional, and a provisional becomes a final invoice. Each new document remembers the one it came from. That chain matters: payments, costs and claims booked on an earlier document still appear on the later one.

Why the chain holds

Every line carries a stable reference that does not change when a document converts. Without it, converting a provisional would silently orphan every cost and claim attached to it.

Status

Draft, confirmed, sent or cancelled. Only confirmed and priced documents count towards receivables, stock and the pickers used elsewhere in the app.

Charges card

A confirmed invoice shows the expenses, revenues and claims booked against it, with a breakdown per good. Each part respects your role: if you cannot see costs, the whole card is hidden rather than shown empty.

Payments

Three tabs: Invoice payments, General payments and Cheques.

How to record an invoice payment

  1. Create the header: person, amount, date, currency, notes. No exchange rate here — the header is a control total.
  2. It opens as a draft.
  3. Add one line per invoice being settled. Each line has its own currency, rate and method.
  4. Confirm. Only now does the money count towards balances.

Splitting across invoice items

Leave the grid at zero and the amount fills automatically from the first item down, each item taking only what it still owes. You can also type the split yourself.

A line can never pay an item more than it owes. The check counts other confirmed payments but ignores the payment you are editing, so re-saving a line does not block itself.

General payments

Money moved with a person that no invoice sits behind. It has a person and an amount, no lines, and rests on that person's account.

Reopening

A confirmed payment can be reopened to a draft. The app warns you first, because reopening pulls that money back out of every balance until you confirm again.

Cheques

A cheque is its own record, not a field on a payment, because one cheque can settle lines on several invoices.

Fields

Type (normal or security), number, issuing bank, owner, due date, amount and currency.

Numbers

Unique per issuing bank

Two different banks may issue the same cheque number, so the check is per bank, not across all of them. The one exception: a returned cheque's number can be entered again, because a bounced cheque is usually replaced by a new one with the same number.

Status

Marking a cheque as paid requires naming which of your accounts received the money. Cheques past their due date are flagged with a warning banner so they are easy to find. A cleared cheque cannot be edited, because its amount is what the payment lines pointing at it are worth.

Transfers

Moving money between your own accounts: bank to cash safe, safe to safe, bank to bank. Across currencies too.

A transfer stands alone

It needs no invoice, no contract and no person. You may link parts of it to invoices afterwards — all of it, some of it, or none. An unlinked remainder is perfectly valid.

The rate

The rate says how many units of the destination currency one unit of the source buys. If both accounts hold the same currency the rate is locked at 1, because anything else would create or destroy money.

Example
From:  CashSafe USD    100 USD
Rate:  3.1
To:    CashSafe IQD    310 IQD

Status

Draft, confirmed or cancelled. Nothing moves until you confirm. A confirmed transfer cannot be cancelled once its account has been revalued, because that would rewrite the balance the gain was measured from.

Exchange gain and loss

When you hold foreign currency and the rate moves, what you hold is worth a different number of dollars. A revaluation records that change.

The worked example

You hold        310 IQD
Carried at      3.1  →  worth $100.00

New rate        3.0  →  worth $103.33

Exchange gain           +$3.33

The rule that matters most

Never count the same gain twice

The "previous rate" is what the balance is currently carried at — not the rate of the transfer that created it. After booking +$3.33 the balance is carried at $103.33, so the next revaluation starts from there.

Revalue again at the same rate and it books zero. Return to the old rate and it books −$3.33. A full round trip nets to nothing.

Three tabs

  • Revaluations — open drafts. The full calculation is shown before you confirm.
  • History — confirmed and cancelled records. Open a row to see what funded the balance: each line names the transfer or the payment it came from.
  • Reports — gain and loss grouped by person, contract, invoice, currency or account.

Tracing a gain back

Where a link exists, the app follows the chain from the gain back to the money that funded the account — a transfer or a payment — and on to the invoice, the contract and the person. Where no link exists — a plain cash safe, for example — the person and contract reports simply stay empty. That is correct: such a gain genuinely has no person behind it, and inventing an "unknown" row would be misleading.

What counts towards an account balance

Three things: confirmed transfers, confirmed payment lines that name the account, and gains already booked.

A payment line is money in when its invoice is a sale and money out when its invoice is a purchase. The direction is read per line, not per payment, so one payment may settle both sides. A bank transfer line counts as soon as the payment is confirmed; a cheque line counts only once the cheque is marked paid, because before that the money is not in the bank. Cash, offset and credit-note lines name no account, so they change no balance.

A line in a currency other than the account's own is skipped, not converted — converting at a rate nobody chose would look right and be wrong. The revaluation screen warns you how many lines were left out, so nothing goes missing quietly.

Realized and unrealized

Unrealized means you still hold the currency and only its value moved. Realized means it was actually converted or settled. Both are supported; revaluation records are unrealized.

Expenses and Revenues

Costs and extra income tied to your trading. Both work the same way and share one implementation — anything true of expenses below is true of revenues.

Two kinds

  • Invoice — attached to one invoice, and spread across its goods.
  • General — company costs such as rent or salaries, with no invoice.

How to record one

  1. Create the document and pick the invoice. The picker offers both purchase and sale invoices, and can be filtered by party, type, date or number.
  2. Open it and add category lines: category, date, amount, currency, rate, person, cost centre, notes.
  3. Each line splits equally across the invoice goods. Change any share by hand, remove a good, or press "re-split equally" to start over.

Rules

  • Every line must name a person.
  • The per-good amounts always add up exactly to the line, and the lines to the document.
  • Only confirmed, priced, latest-in-chain invoices can be chosen.
  • A cancelled document is excluded from every total.
Cents

Splitting $100 across three goods gives 33.34 / 33.33 / 33.33 — never 33.33 three times. The odd cent goes to the first line so the parts always add up exactly.

Claims

A claim records damage or a shortfall against one invoice.

  • Two sides: an expense claim is raised on a purchase invoice, a revenue claim on a sale invoice.
  • Two types: quantity or quality.
  • Pick one invoice, then enter an amount and a reason per good. Only goods with an amount are saved.
  • The header total is the sum of the items and cannot be typed.

The person on a claim comes from the invoice, never from a dropdown, so a claim cannot be filed against the wrong party.

An account's currency cannot change after you create it, because that currency defines what its balance means and every transfer already booked against it.

Bank accounts require an account number and IBAN. Cash safes do not, and any bank-only field typed by mistake is discarded rather than stored.

Reports

A summary view of trading and receivables: what is invoiced, what is collected, what is overdue, and how old the unpaid amounts are.

Exchange gain and loss has its own reports inside the Exchange screen, grouped five different ways.

Settings

Theme, language, company name, base currency, and the default exchange rates for AED and IQD.

Changing a rate is safe

These rates are only the values offered when you create something new. Every saved record keeps the rate that was actually used, so editing this never rewrites history.

Danger zone holds Load sample data and Reset. Both replace everything and reload the page.

Customer portal

A separate, limited view for one linked customer. They see only their own contracts, invoices, payments and balance — never anyone else's, and never the internal screens.

The link is set from the person's record, and only one person can hold it at a time.

How it is built

Technology

React 18 with TypeScript, Vite, Ant Design 5, TanStack Query, Zustand and react-i18next. Three languages with full right-to-left support, plus light and dark themes.

Where the data lives

There is no server. All data is kept in the browser's local storage, behind a small service layer. To connect a real backend later, only that layer needs replacing — the screens stay as they are.

Layers

screens  →  query hooks  →  service layer  →  stored data

Screens never reach the data directly. Every total, split and check happens in the service layer, so the same rule cannot be written twice and drift apart.

Upgrading safely

When something new is added, a database saved by an older version must still load. New lists fill in as empty, and missing fields fall back to a safe meaning. This is tested with hand-built copies of old databases before each release.

Checking the work

  • Type check, linting and a production build must all pass.
  • Rules are proved by running them, not by reading the code.
  • Screens are opened in a real browser, in all three languages, with the console watched for errors.
  • Text keys are compared across the three language files so none can go missing.
