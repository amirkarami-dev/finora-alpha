Basics

Money

The app supports USD, AED and IQD. USD is the base currency: every total you compare or report on is a US dollar figure.

A foreign-currency record always stores the rate that was used at that moment. Changing a rate in Settings never rewrites old records — that rate is only the value offered when you create something new.

Rates are written as units per one dollar. So 3.6725 means 3.6725 AED buys one dollar.

Draft and confirmed

Many documents start as a draft. A draft is visible but does not affect any balance, total or report. It only counts once you confirm it. Invoices, payments and transfers all work this way.

Deactivate, not delete

Master records — persons, goods, categories, accounts — are never deleted. You deactivate them. They stop appearing in new documents, and every old document that used them stays exactly as it was.

Starting empty

A fresh install has no data at all. To explore with realistic figures, use Settings → Danger zone → Load sample data. Reset clears everything again.

Dashboard

The opening screen for Manager and Staff. It shows total outstanding, overdue, collected and invoiced, plus charts for money in against money out, product mix, contract status and how old the unpaid amounts are.

Every figure counts confirmed money only. A draft payment does not appear here.

CEO users see a separate Executive screen instead, with the same story at a higher level.

Persons

Everyone you deal with: buyers, suppliers, employees and others. This list used to be called Customers; the name was widened because it now holds more than customers.

Types

TypeMeaningCan sign contracts?
BuyerYou sell to themSale contracts
SupplierYou buy from themPurchase contracts
Buyer & supplierBothBoth
EmployeeWorks for youNo
OtherAnyone else, such as an authorityNo

Employees and Others do not trade, so the form hides currency, payment terms, credit limit and portal access, and explains why. They never appear when you choose a party for a contract.

Balance

One number for each person, counting buying and selling together:

  + sale invoices
  - payments received on sales
  - sale claims
  - purchase invoices
  + purchase claims
  + payments made on purchases
  ------------------------------
  = balance

Positive means they owe you. A number in brackets, like (390), means you owe them. A person who both buys and sells gets one net figure — their two sides simply cancel out.

Click the balance to see why it is what it is

Every movement behind it, oldest first, each with the amount, what it did to the balance, and the running balance after it. The headline figure is the last running value, so the number and its explanation cannot drift apart.

For somebody who both buys and sells, the panel also totals the two sides separately, so "they owe 1,000, we owe 1,390, net (390)" is visible at a glance.

A cheque that has not cleared is listed but counts zero, marked not counted yet. Transfers linked to that person's invoices are listed for information and count zero too — they move your money between your own accounts.

Balance is not the same as Outstanding

Balance is the two-sided figure above, and it can go negative. Outstanding is the older sale-only receivable, never below zero, and it is what ageing, days-to-collect and credit limits are built on — ageing a purchase invoice would mean nothing. Both are shown; they answer different questions.

The person page

Four tabs: Contracts, Invoices, Claims and Payments. Invoices shows buying and selling together, because the same person can be both. Claims shows what was raised against them; it is tied to the invoice, so a claim can never appear under the wrong person.

Portal access

One person at a time can be linked to the customer portal. Turning it on for someone automatically turns it off for anyone else. Deactivating a person also removes their portal link.

Contracts

A contract is an agreement with one person to buy or sell goods. It holds one or more goods lines.

Pricing

Each goods line is priced from the metal exchange:

unit price = LME price × (LME percent ÷ 100) + premium
line value = unit price × quantity in tonnes

The LME percent reflects how much metal the material actually contains. The premium is what you add on top.

Prices belong to the deal, not the product

LME percent, premium and incoterm sit on the contract line, never on the goods record. The same copper sold to two buyers carries two different premiums.

Quantity limit

You cannot invoice more than the contract agreed. If you try, the message shows the contract quantity, what is already invoiced, what is left and what you asked for. Provisional invoices count towards the limit.

Containers

A container is a shipment record and nothing more. It holds a reference, load date, gross and net weight, and the goods inside.

It carries no money, no status and no contract link. How much has shipped is worked out from the invoices instead, so there is only one place that decides it.

Warehouse

Goods in and goods out. Each document is linked to a confirmed, priced invoice and moves stock into or out of one warehouse.

Stock per warehouse and per product is calculated from these documents. A document that would push stock below zero is refused.

Warehouses themselves are master records you can deactivate.

Purchase and Sale

Six document types, three on each side:

StagePurchaseSaleHas prices?
OrderPurchase orderSale orderNo
ProvisionalPurchase provisionalSale provisionalYes
FinalPurchase invoiceSale invoiceYes

The chain

An order becomes a provisional, and a provisional becomes a final invoice. Each new document remembers the one it came from. That chain matters: payments, costs and claims booked on an earlier document still appear on the later one.

Why the chain holds

Every line carries a stable reference that does not change when a document converts. Without it, converting a provisional would silently orphan every cost and claim attached to it.

Status

Draft, confirmed, sent or cancelled. Only confirmed and priced documents count towards receivables, stock and the pickers used elsewhere in the app.

Charges card

A confirmed invoice shows the expenses, revenues and claims booked against it, with a breakdown per good. Each part respects your role: if you cannot see costs, the whole card is hidden rather than shown empty.

Payments

Three tabs: Invoice payments, General payments and Cheques.

How to record an invoice payment

  1. Create the header: person, amount, date, currency, notes. No exchange rate here — the header is a control total.
  2. It opens as a draft.
  3. Add one line per invoice being settled. Each line has its own currency, rate and method.
  4. Confirm. Only now does the money count towards balances.

Splitting across invoice items

Leave the grid at zero and the amount fills automatically from the first item down, each item taking only what it still owes. You can also type the split yourself.

A line can never pay an item more than it owes. The check counts other confirmed payments but ignores the payment you are editing, so re-saving a line does not block itself.

Methods

Each method asks for what it actually needs, and nothing more.

MethodAlso needs
TT (bank transfer)Which bank account
CashWhich cash safe
ChequeThe cheque details — you write a new cheque here
Offset, Credit noteNothing extra

A bank transfer must name a bank and a cash payment must name a safe. Pointing one at the other is refused, because otherwise the two would be impossible to tell apart in any balance.

Cheques are written here, not picked from a list

Choosing Cheque opens the cheque fields on the line itself. That gives every cheque in the register one origin story: it exists because somebody was paid with it.

The cheque is only created once the whole line passes its checks. If the line is refused, no cheque is left behind and its number stays free. Editing a line that already has a cheque shows it read-only and points you at the register, where its status rules live.

General payments

Money moved with a person that no invoice sits behind — money on account. It carries lines exactly like an invoice payment, but the lines name no document, so there is nothing to split them across.

You say which way it went

An invoice payment works out its own direction: money comes in on a sale and goes out on a purchase. Money on account has no document to ask, so the header asks you — money in, or money out. Guessing would put every supplier prepayment on the wrong side of that person's balance.

Reopening

A confirmed payment can be reopened to a draft. The app warns you first, because reopening pulls that money back out of every balance until you confirm again.

Cheques

A cheque is its own record, not a field on a payment, because one cheque can settle lines on several invoices.

Cheques are written on a payment line (see Payments above). This tab lists them, filters them, edits them and moves them through their statuses — it does not create them.

Fields

Type (normal or security), number, issuing bank, owner, due date, amount and currency.

Finding one

Filter by status, or search the number, owner and issuing bank at once. You can also narrow by type, currency, due-date range and amount range, or show only the ones that need action — past their due date and still not cleared.

Numbers

Unique per issuing bank

Two different banks may issue the same cheque number, so the check is per bank, not across all of them. The one exception: a returned cheque's number can be entered again, because a bounced cheque is usually replaced by a new one with the same number.

Status

StatusMeaning
PendingWaiting to clear
PaidCleared into one of your accounts
ReturnedBounced
ExpiredPast due, never cleared
ChangedReplaced by another cheque

Any status can follow any other. Paid and Changed used to be dead ends, which made a mistyped cheque unfixable and left no way to undo a cheque marked paid by mistake. What is guarded now is the money, not the list of allowed moves.

To correct a cheque that is already paid

Move it back to pending, edit it, then mark it paid again. Data can only be changed while a cheque is pending — that two-step makes changing a settled record something you decide to do, not something that happens because you opened a form.

Moving out of paid also clears the bank it was paid into. Otherwise an uncleared cheque would keep reporting a bank, and re-paying it somewhere else could quietly keep the old one.

Marking a cheque as paid requires naming which of your accounts received the money, because paid is the moment the money lands. Cheques past their due date are flagged with a warning banner so they are easy to find.

A cheque counts only when it is paid

Until then it is a promise. It shows on the account and on the person's balance detail, marked as not counted yet, and moves neither figure. Mark it paid and both move; move it back to pending and both move back.

Transfers

Moving money between your own accounts: bank to cash safe, safe to safe, bank to bank. Across currencies too.

A transfer stands alone

It needs no invoice, no contract and no person. You may link parts of it to invoices afterwards — all of it, some of it, or none. An unlinked remainder is perfectly valid.

The rate

The rate says how many units of the destination currency one unit of the source buys. If both accounts hold the same currency the rate is locked at 1, because anything else would create or destroy money.

Example
From:  CashSafe USD    100 USD
Rate:  3.1
To:    CashSafe IQD    310 IQD

Status

Draft, confirmed or cancelled. Nothing moves until you confirm, and a confirmed transfer can be cancelled again — which reverses both balances. It used to be locked once its account had been revalued; gain and loss no longer read account balances at all, so there is nothing left to protect.

Where they show up

In the Banks & cash safes report as movements on both accounts. If you linked part of a transfer to an invoice, it also appears in that person's balance detail, marked for information — moving money between your own accounts never changes what anybody owes you.

Exchange gain and loss

When you hold foreign currency and the rate moves, what you hold is worth a different number of dollars. This is where you write that down.

It is a plain record, on purpose

Date, gain or loss, amount, notes. Nothing else — no account, no rate, no balance.

This replaced a revaluation engine that carried book rates per account, showed a preview before confirming, split each gain proportionally back across the transfers and payments that had funded the account, and reported it five different ways. It was a great deal of machinery answering a question nobody was asking. What the desk wanted was to record what the currency cost or earned.

Adding one

  1. Choose Gain or Loss on the toggle.
  2. Enter the amount as a plain positive number. The toggle carries the direction, so the field refuses a minus sign.
  3. Set the date, add notes if useful, and save.

A line under the amount confirms what will happen — "saved as a loss, it lowers your net". Asking somebody to express a loss by remembering to type a minus sign is a trap, and a mistyped sign would look exactly like a real entry.

The list

Three totals across the top — gain, loss and net — then every record with its number, date, type, amount and notes. Records can be edited or deleted. Editing the toggle flips the sign; the totals follow.

Deleting really deletes. Everywhere else in Finora cancels instead, because other records point at the thing being cancelled. Nothing points at a gain or loss entry, so leaving struck-through rows behind would be clutter with no reason behind it.

What counts towards an account balance

Two things: confirmed transfers, and confirmed payment lines that name the account. Gains and losses are not among them — they are notes about currency, not movements on any particular account.

A payment line is money in when its invoice is a sale and money out when its invoice is a purchase. The direction is read per line, not per payment, so one payment may settle both sides. Money on account uses the direction you chose on the header. A bank or cash line counts as soon as the payment is confirmed; a cheque line counts only once the cheque is marked paid, because before that the money is not in the bank. Offset and credit-note lines name no account, so they change no balance.

A line in a currency other than the account's own is skipped, not converted — converting at a rate nobody chose would look right and be wrong.

Expenses and Revenues

Costs and extra income tied to your trading. Both work the same way and share one implementation — anything true of expenses below is true of revenues.

Two kinds

  • Invoice — attached to one invoice, and spread across its goods.
  • General — company costs such as rent or salaries, with no invoice.

How to record one

  1. Create the document and pick the invoice. The picker offers both purchase and sale invoices, and can be filtered by party, type, date or number.
  2. Open it and add category lines: category, date, amount, currency, rate, person, cost centre, notes.
  3. Each line splits equally across the invoice goods. Change any share by hand, remove a good, or press "re-split equally" to start over.

Rules

  • Every line must name a person.
  • The per-good amounts always add up exactly to the line, and the lines to the document.
  • Only confirmed, priced, latest-in-chain invoices can be chosen.
  • A cancelled document is excluded from every total.
Cents

Splitting $100 across three goods gives 33.34 / 33.33 / 33.33 — never 33.33 three times. The odd cent goes to the first line so the parts always add up exactly.

Claims

A claim records damage or a shortfall against one invoice.

  • Two sides, each named after the document it is filed against: a sale claim sits on a sale invoice, a purchase claim on a purchase invoice.
  • Two types: quantity or quality.
  • Pick one invoice, then enter an amount and a reason per good. Only goods with an amount are saved.
  • The header total is the sum of the items and cannot be typed.

The person on a claim comes from the invoice, never from a dropdown, so a claim cannot be filed against the wrong party.

The sides were renamed, and it was not just a label

They used to be called expense and revenue, and they pointed at the opposite document: an "expense claim" was filed against a purchase invoice. Simply renaming would have re-pointed every saved claim at the wrong kind of document — and because a person's balance now counts the two sides in opposite directions, that would have silently flipped each claim's sign.

So the app does not go by the old label at all. On first load it reads each claim's own invoice and sets the side from that. A claim whose stored side already disagreed with its invoice is corrected rather than preserved.

Effect on a person's balance

A sale claim is credit you granted, so it lowers what that person owes you. A purchase claim is credit they granted you, so it lowers what you owe them — which raises the net.

Bank accounts require an account number and IBAN. Cash safes do not, and any bank-only field typed by mistake is discarded rather than stored.

Five tabs. The first is the charts; the other four are movement reports.

The date range

One range across all four, so switching tabs keeps the window you are looking at. It starts at the last twelve months. Overview has no picker, because its charts are fixed windows of their own and a control that changed nothing would only mislead.

Opening plus movements always equals closing

The range applies to the movements; the opening balance is everything before it. That is what makes the three figures agree by construction rather than by luck.

The Persons report takes its opening from the running balance of the last row before the window, rather than re-adding the amounts itself. Two ways of computing the same number are two chances to disagree.

Settings

Theme, language, company name, base currency, and the default exchange rates for AED and IQD.

Changing a rate is safe

These rates are only the values offered when you create something new. Every saved record keeps the rate that was actually used, so editing this never rewrites history.

Danger zone holds Load sample data and Reset. Both replace everything and reload the page.

Customer portal

A separate, limited view for one linked customer. They see only their own contracts, invoices, payments and balance — never anyone else's, and never the internal screens.

The link is set from the person's record, and only one person can hold it at a time.

How it is built

Technology

React 18 with TypeScript, Vite, Ant Design 5, TanStack Query, Zustand and react-i18next. Three languages with full right-to-left support, plus light and dark themes.

Where the data lives

There is no server. All data is kept in the browser's local storage, behind a small service layer. To connect a real backend later, only that layer needs replacing — the screens stay as they are.

Layers

screens  →  query hooks  →  service layer  →  stored data

Screens never reach the data directly. Every total, split and check happens in the service layer, so the same rule cannot be written twice and drift apart.

Upgrading safely

When something new is added, a database saved by an older version must still load. New lists fill in as empty, and missing fields fall back to a safe meaning. This is tested with hand-built copies of old databases before each release.

Checking the work

  • Type check, linting and a production build must all pass.
  • Rules are proved by running them, not by reading the code.
  • Screens are opened in a real browser, in all three languages, with the console watched for errors.
  • Text keys are compared across the three language files so none can go missing.
