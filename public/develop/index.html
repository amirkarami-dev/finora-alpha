Money

Payments with a header and lines

A payment now has a header (person, amount, date, currency, notes) and settlement lines. Each line settles one invoice and carries its own currency, rate and method. A payment starts as a draft and only counts towards balances once you confirm it. You can reopen a confirmed payment to change it.

Automatic split across invoice items

Leave the grid empty and the amount fills from the first item down, each item taking only what it still owes. You can also type the split yourself. The system refuses to pay an item more than it owes.

Each payment method asks for what it needs

A bank transfer names a bank account; a cash payment names a cash safe. Pointing one at the other is refused — otherwise the two would be impossible to tell apart in any balance. This also fixed a real gap: a confirmed cash payment used to move no cash safe at all.

Cheques are written on the payment line

Choosing Cheque opens the cheque fields there and then, instead of picking from a list. Every cheque in the register then has one origin story: somebody was paid with it. The cheque is only created once the whole line passes, so a rejected line leaves nothing behind and does not burn the cheque number.

Done

Cheque register

Cheques are their own records, because one cheque can settle several invoices. Every status can follow every other — Paid and Changed used to be dead ends, so a mistyped cheque was unfixable. To correct a paid cheque, move it back to pending, edit, then pay it again; moving out of paid also clears the bank it went into. Marking one paid asks which of your accounts it cleared through. Filters cover number, owner, bank, status, type, currency, due date and amount.

General payments carry lines too

Money on account settles no particular invoice, so its lines name no document and there is nothing to split them across. You say which way it went — in or out — because there is no invoice to work it out from. Before this, the Add line button was hidden on general payments, so there was no way to record anything at all.

You write the cheque, so you pick the bank

On a line paying money out, the issuing bank is chosen from your own accounts instead of typed — you are the one writing that cheque. A cheque you were given is drawn on the payer's bank, which is not yours, so it stays free text. Which way a line's money goes is read the same way the balances read it: from the line's own invoice, or from the header when there is none. Reading the header alone would have called every supplier payment money coming in.

A person's balance counts both sides

One figure per person: sale invoices, less what they paid, less sale claims, less purchase invoices, plus purchase claims, plus what you paid them. Positive means they owe you; a number in brackets means you owe them. Somebody who both buys and sells gets one net figure instead of two half-answers.

Costs booked against a person count in their balance

An expense line naming a person now reduces their balance, and a revenue line raises it, exactly as an invoice would. Before this, money you spent on somebody's behalf sat in the expense report and nowhere else, so their balance was quietly short by that amount. Each line counts on its own, so one document spread across three people lands on three balances.

Account balances count transfers and payments

A bank or cash safe balance is built from confirmed transfers and from confirmed payment lines that name that account. The direction comes from the invoice on the line: money from a sale comes in, money for a purchase goes out. A bank or cash line counts at once; a cheque counts only after you mark it paid. A line in another currency is skipped, never converted at a rate nobody chose.

Claims are named after the document they sit on

A sale claim is filed on a sale invoice, a purchase claim on a purchase invoice. The old names pointed at the opposite document, so a plain rename would have re-pointed every saved claim at the wrong kind — and since the two sides now count in opposite directions on a person's balance, that would have silently flipped each claim's sign. Instead the app ignores the old label and reads each claim's own invoice.
