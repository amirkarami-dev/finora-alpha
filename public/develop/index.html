Money

Payments with a header and lines

A payment now has a header (person, amount, date, currency, notes) and settlement lines. Each line settles one invoice and carries its own currency, rate and method. A payment starts as a draft and only counts towards balances once you confirm it. You can reopen a confirmed payment to change it.

Cheque register

Cheques are their own records, because one cheque can settle several invoices. A cheque moves through Pending, Paid, Returned, Expired or Changed. Marking one as paid asks which of your accounts received the money. Cheques past their due date are flagged.

Exchange gain and loss

No double counting when you revalue again

Each revaluation measures from what the balance is currently carried at, not from the original transfer. Revalue twice at the same rate and the second one books zero. Return to the old rate and it books the exact opposite. A whole history nets to zero.

Done

Account balances count transfers and payments

A bank or cash safe balance is built from confirmed transfers and from confirmed payment lines that name that account. The direction comes from the invoice on the line: money from a sale comes in, money for a purchase goes out. A bank transfer counts at once; a cheque counts only after you mark it paid. A line in another currency is skipped, never converted at a rate nobody chose, and the revaluation screen warns you how many were left out.

Master data

Goods list

The products you trade, with name, code, metal type, form, unit and customs code. They are suggested when you add goods to a contract, so names stay spelled the same way everywhere. Deactivating a good hides it from new contracts but keeps old ones intact.
