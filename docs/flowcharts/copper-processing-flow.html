Copper cable → ingot → sale, in Jalil-Jalal

the flow, step by step
The one rule: the money you paid never disappears with the lost weight. $10,000 stays $10,000 and moves onto fewer tons, so the cost per MT goes up at every step. The app counts in metric tons (MT): 1,000 kg = 1.000 MT, and it keeps 6 decimals, so 0.5 kg = 0.0005 MT.

If the workshop has its own costs (usual case)

Stripping and melting cost money too: workers, electricity, gas. Add them to the copper at the step where they happen — into the stock, not into expenses, until the copper is sold.

Insulation sold as scrap (say $200)? Take it off the copper: stripped copper = $9,800 → $15,077 / MT. Or book it as a revenue and leave the copper at $10,000. Both are fine — pick one and keep it.

How the app does it

Warehouse › Conversions

  • Conversion document, numbered CNV-2026-0001: warehouse, date, input lines (goods, MT), output lines (goods, MT), cost lines (category, person, amount). Staff makes the draft; a Manager clicks Confirm. Confirm checks the stock of every input, moves the cost onto the outputs, and books the cost lines as an expense by itself.
  • Stored cost per MT on every stock item, per warehouse. A receipt sets it from the purchase invoice's price; a conversion carries it forward onto what comes out. The Inventory tab shows it.
  • Cost of sales on the sale invoice: once the goods are confirmed out, the invoice shows what they cost and the margin, by itself.
